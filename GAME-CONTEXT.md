# The Shop That Opens Once: game context

The single source of truth for what this game is, what has been decided, and what is still open. Read it before any full-game design, story or development task.

Last updated: 2026-10-21.

**Status:** building the full game in Unity, on the new design in the UTS GDD. The following exist:
- the UTS GDD, which redesigns the story and gameplay (`docs/game-design-document.pdf`)
- the master script and the world and character bible, which update the GDD's story, world and characters (`docs/The_Shop_That_Opens_Once_ULTIMATE_30PAGE_SCRIPT.pdf`, `docs/The_Shop_That_Opens_Once_WORLD_AND_CHARACTER_BIBLE.pdf`, both in Indonesian)
- the earlier concept proposal and the feasibility report
- the Unity project in `game/` with the foundation built: the Godot core ported to C# (places, walking, talk, the bag, keepsakes, saving, sound), the Prologue (Day 0) as the game's first chapter, a systems-test chapter that is not part of the story (an editor-only title option, until Night 1 takes it over), and playthrough tests of both that pass
- the art pipeline in `game/ArtSource/`, exporting into `game/Assets/Resources/Art/`. Bellwood outdoors is five small areas joined by edge exits (the station, the banyan grove, the square, the east and the coast; `areas.js`, drawn with the `village*.js` kits) in a cozy RPG-village style, with wind, cloud shadows and bugs. The game shows them in an HD-2D 3D view (raised ground, upright cards, tilt-shift blur and bloom), graded for the time of day (design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-bellwood-world-overhaul-design.md`, look and layout revised the same day). The nine interiors follow, cut open where their houses stand (`inside.js`): Satria's childhood home and Marlow's shop so far.
- the dev log of each step, in Bahasa Indonesia, in `docs/devlog/`
- the web presentation, frozen. It still tells the earlier story.
- the Godot MVP (Nights 1 and 2 of the earlier story), archived in `archive/godot-mvp/`

## How to use this file

1. Read **Decisions** and **Open questions** first. You are done when you know which open questions your task depends on.
2. Ask the user about each open question your task depends on before designing around it. Label anything you invent `[Illustrative]` until the user adopts it.
3. When the user settles a question, move it into **Decisions** with the date. You are done when the two lists agree.

## Decisions

**Sources**
- `[Decision]` The UTS GDD defines the world, story, characters, mechanics and the seven nights. It replaces the concept proposal, whose story (Arthur Tate, the 18:17 clock, Marlow's sister Elise, Sam as a child) is retired. The report still defines feasibility and scope. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` The master script and the world and character bible update the GDD and win over it where they differ. Between the two, the script wins for story, scenes and dialogue, and the bible for the world, places, interiors and the characters' details. The Decisions here win over all three: the ant planet, bugs only, the HD-2D top-down view and the controls stay, though the script and the bible have human relics at a 1:50 scale, roosters, sparrows and gulls, a car and a telephone, side-scrolling A/D movement, a 480x270 canvas and E/I keys. (2026-10-19)
- `[Decision]` The storm was in October 1952 and the story is set in 1972, twenty years later, as the script and GDD 5 have it (the bible's "74 years" and its eras up to 2025 are not followed). Satria left for the city eleven years ago, at 17, so he is about 28; in 1952 he was a small boy, on the train's ticket register with his parents. Ratih died two years ago. (2026-10-19)
- `[Decision]` Satria is he/him, the only son (*putra*) of Daniel and Ratih, as both new docs have it. Daniel is Satria's father: the GDD's separate "Ayah Satria" and "Daniel, Theo's friend" are one person. (2026-10-19)
- `[Decision]` Helen waited for Marcus Bell, the station master who is Marlow: she was his fiancée, Night 1's watch is his, and on Night 6 Marlow, unmasked, leaves Satria a last message of love for her (the bible). The script's "Arthur", a separate train driver, is not followed: it is the retired story's name. (2026-10-19)
- `[Decision]` The city property agent is Valdo (Agen Valdo, of the firm Bram Capital), as the bible names him; Kapten Bram, the retired conductor, is the only Bram. The script's "Pak Bram" lines for the agent become "Pak Valdo". (2026-10-19)

**Engine and builds**
- `[Decision]` The engine is Unity 6.6 (6000.6.6f1) with C#. It replaces Godot 4. The editor is VS Code. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` The game ships as desktop builds for Windows and macOS, into `game/Builds/Windows` and `game/Builds/macOS` (ignored by git). The web build is cancelled: with the full HD-2D look (Bokeh depth of field, bloom, HDR, shadows over a full-window view) it ran at about 6 fps in Chrome on an Apple M4, while the same scene costs about 2.4 ms a frame natively, and reaching 60 on the web would have meant rendering less. The user chose the full look over the web. (2026-10-20)
- `[Decision]` Builds keep every post-processing effect: URP's stripping of unused post-processing variants is off, because the game builds its depth of field, bloom and vignette in code (`Main.Effects`), so no volume asset in the project shows them to the stripper and a build would drop them. (2026-10-20)
- `[Decision]` The courier keeps an errand: the story's last hint, which used to show for four seconds only, stays at the top of the bag's notes as "Now: ...", in Satria's words, until the next one. The user got stuck on Night 1's dusk (the platform's lanterns) after missing the one hint that said so. It is a line in his notes, not a marker or an arrow, so the no-markers pillar holds; the how-to-play tips stay passing toasts. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The nights' days are asked round, as the user chose (they found the nights repetitive, each one a single person to find). By day the courier shows the night's keepsake to whoever he talks to, the first time he comes to them with it, and each villager says what they make of it in their own voice; the ones who know something give a lead, which goes into the bag's notes. The story's own leads and the keepsake's owner keep their scenes instead. Hints never name the next person before someone in the story has: the morning hint asks him to show the keepsake round. Nights 6 and 7, which are not a search for an owner, have no reactions. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The nights' days also carry post, the second of the user's four picks against repetitive nights. On Nights 1 to 5, two villagers each ask the courier, the first time he talks to them by day, to take a letter or a parcel to someone across the village (Mia's thanks to Nora for the shawl, the porter's tea for Helen, the Baker's rolls for Sam, and so on). It is optional: "Not today, sorry." leaves it, and it is not asked again. Taken, it rides in the bag like a keepsake (an envelope or the parcel art, one side), and handing it over is a short scene with the recipient's thanks; `delivered_<id>` is remembered across chapters for the village to recall later. Deliveries never gate the story, never point to the night's owner, and are never named in the hints. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The village remembers, the third of the user's picks against repetitive nights. The day after each handover (Nights 2 to 5; Leo's on Nights 6 and 7), five or six villagers have a word of it in their own voice (Helen humming at dawn, Pip paying for the rabbit's roll, Theo filming the boats again, Sam opening the hall ten minutes late, Leo running flat out), and a delivered parcel is recalled by its sender or recipient on the next day or two (`Gossip`). Each word is said once, the first time the courier talks to them on its days, before their own lines, and never by a villager the night's story needs. What a handover changes shows too: from Night 3 Pip keeps Mochi in hand (`Person.Holds`), and lines that a handover makes untrue are replaced from the next night (Theo's locked darkroom, Leo's "don't time me", Pip's fear of thunder). (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` Pace and passing life, the last of the user's four picks against repetitive nights. On Nights 2 to 5 the morning plays on from waking as part of the montage: straight to the stove for the tea, with a breakfast someone left after the last handover (Helen's tea leaves "With sugar. H.", Mia's carrot loaf knotted by Pip, Theo's smoked fish and Daniel's mint, the Baker's seed loaf with Sam's note), which is decision 129's "breakfast is what the village gave him" made real; Night 1 and Nights 6 and 7 keep the stove as their own press. Once the tea is drunk the bag's errand moves on to the day's first place instead of staying on the stove. The montage's familiarity line is a different one each night, and the mat's line is said on the first night only. Turning to the courier as he passes, a villager now and then says a word of hello in a small paper balloon over their head (`Hud.Bark`): by the time of day, the children's own, the rain's; two at most at once, and only while the courier is free to walk. No new animals: the ant-scale bugs already fly and crawl in every outdoor place (decisions 94 and 162). (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The UI gets richer, without breaking the no-markers pillar, as the user asked (they found the HUD and the chapter card too plain). Every chapter card is a courier's postcard: it slides up on the cream, in an airmail border, addressed to Satria, Bellwood, its title at 22 px (twice the font's grid); its stamp holds the night's keepsake (Marlow's lantern for the prologue and the epilogue), and a blue BELLWOOD postmark with the day thumps down on it. The time-moving cards are the postmark alone over their lines. Night cards now also say "Day N, midnight." The HUD gains moments rather than widgets: coming into a place, its name (the bible's zones: Bellwood Station, The Market Square, The East Gardens, The East Road, The South Coast, The Banyan Grove, and each interior) drops in on a wooden sign hung by two ropes at the top centre, hangs three seconds and goes back up; the bag hops when something new goes in it and wears a red dot until it is opened. The one standing addition, the user's pick among ideas beyond the GDD: through Nights 1 to 7, Marlow's seven lanterns hang from a banyan branch under the day dial, flickering, and each goes out in a wisp of smoke when its keepsake is home (`Game.LanternsOut`, as in the shop); hovering them says how many still burn. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The UI's text is Pixelify Sans at 11 px, the size its pixel grid is drawn at, imported as hinted raster (no smoothing), so every letter is whole canvas pixels and stays crisp at any scale. At the earlier 13 px its pixels fell between the canvas's and were smoothed into grey edges, which the user found blurry and hard to read at 4K, and names sat low in their tags. The user tried 11 against changing the font and kept Pixelify. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` Past 1080p the world is drawn at 1080p's detail and scaled up by a whole number with no smoothing (URP's render scale at 1 / (factor / 3), point upscaling), so a 4K screen draws the same pixels as 1080p, four times over; the UI stays at the window's full resolution. On an Apple M4 the macOS build fell to about 20 fps at 4K fullscreen, the shadows and depth of field costing four times as much for no detail the art has (it is drawn at 3 screen pixels to an art pixel at 1080p). The user chose this over a smaller shadow map, which would have changed the picture. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` Build targets: Web first, then Windows. Those two build modules are installed. (2026-10-18, superseded 2026-10-20)
- `[Decision]` The Godot MVP is an archive and stays untouched. It lives in `archive/godot-mvp/`, and the git tag `godot-mvp` marks its last commit. Its web build stays live at https://the-shop-that-opens-once-game.vercel.app as a record. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` The Unity project lives in `game/`. It started from the Universal 2D template (URP with the 2D renderer), trimmed to sprites, the Input System, uGUI and the Test Framework. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` Before Unity: the engine was Godot 4.7.2 with GDScript, because C# projects in Godot 4 cannot export to the web. (2026-09-28, superseded 2026-10-18)

Places are C# data (`Places.cs`); each chapter's story is a C# class. The world and UI draw into a small canvas that is scaled up by a whole number. (2026-10-18)

- `[Decision]` `.mcp.json` connects to the server the Unity package runs, over HTTP at `http://127.0.0.1:8080/mcp`, instead of starting its own over stdio. Open the project in Unity first. (2026-10-18)

Each milestone gets a git tag. The project is a course assignment, and the log records its progress. (2026-10-18)

**The world overhaul** (design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-bellwood-world-overhaul-design.md`, approved 2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` Bellwood is an **ant planet**. No humans exist and nothing human is left behind. Ants build everything from nature (twigs, bark, leaves, walnut and acorn shells, mushrooms, pebbles, clay, reeds) and from their own crafts (woodwork, stone, iron from the forge, glass). The railway, the train, the station clock and the lighthouse are ant-made. This overrides the GDD's "human leftovers" (selling point 1.5 and design pillar 4): human leftovers would raise questions the story never answers. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` (superseded 2026-10-18, see the look below) The look is natural materials: walnut-shell and acorn houses, leaf roofs, twig fences, mushroom street lamps, pebble paths, and clover, dandelions and ferns towering over the roofs so the ants feel small. Each zone has its own main materials and colours, and no two buildings share both their shape and their main material. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` The world must never feel empty: no plain patch without a purpose bigger than about a third of the view, forest, fields, water or rock between places instead of lawn, and something moving on every screen. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` (superseded 2026-10-18, see the areas below) The world grows to about 3 times today's width and height, so walking past everything takes minutes, filled with new sub-areas rather than stretched. Buildings grow too: a home is 3–4 ants tall. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` (revised 2026-10-21, see the pace below) Holding Shift makes the courier run. No fast travel and no map. Story places sit close to the main road and the bridges; the extra exploring space lies off to the sides. (2026-10-18)
- `[Decision]` The courier's pace is a switch, not a key held, as the user asked: a tap of Shift turns running on or off, a small note says "Running." or "Walking.", and the pace is kept for the next session. Indoors the courier always walks (a room is a few steps across and seen close), and a tap there says he will run once outdoors. No stamina bar: with no running timer and nothing to miss (decision 132), running costs the player nothing worth limiting, and a draining bar would be a small timer against the GDD's no-timer pillar. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The settings page, now that the game is finished (decision 141 kept sound on/off until then): a paper page with "Settings" on a nailed wood plate, opened from the pause menu (in place of sound on/off) and from the title. The music's, the sounds' (effects, wind and ambience) and the voices' volumes are ten pips each, heard as the pips' share squared; text speed is Slow, Normal or Fast (a factor on each speaker's own pace and pauses); the screen is fullscreen or a window. W / S or the mouse picks a row, A / D or a click changes it, a click on a pip sets the volume there, Esc or Back closes it. Everything is kept between sessions. This settles the open question of text speed and gives decision 129 its voice volume. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` Developer keys, in the editor and development builds only, to try the story out: F5 moves the time of day on (as before), F6 wins the minigame on screen, F7 held types each line out and moves it on (and moves cards, journal pages and reveals on), and F8 ends the chapter as its end would, starting the next. Normal builds have none of them. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The title grows with the save, the user's pick among ideas (their own was the cast lined up): the grove before Marlow's door shows Satria on the step and, close round him, each one whose keepsake he has brought home so far (Helen after Night 1, Mia and Pip with the mended Mochi after Night 2, Theo with his camera, Sam, Leo), Marlow in his doorway after Night 6, and once the epilogue's credits have rolled the village a ring further out (Mender, Baker, Nora, Bram). A new game shows Satria alone, so nothing of the story is shown before it is played. They keep their small life as villagers do. The menu is a wooden sign hung by two ropes from a banyan branch, dropping in as it opens, the line picked in full paint with Marlow's lantern flickering by it; it fits on screen. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` A cinematic scene steps the camera closer only outdoors; in a room cut open it keeps the room's own closeness (twice the outdoor view, decision 109), with the bars. On top of the room's zoom, the step closer let a blurred wall or the trees round the house fill half the screen in Satria's home (waking in bed, the mat, the stove), Theo's studio and the vault, and put the clock's face and the lanterns off the top of the screen as the lantern goes out at a handover. That shot now looks between the clock and the cabinet, so the whole back wall shows: the clock's face moving on a minute, the seven lanterns and their boxes. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` Valdo's deadline is the morning of Day Eight throughout: on Day 0 he no longer says they sign "the day after tomorrow", and on Night 2 the investors come "at the week's end". (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The game speaks English or Bahasa Indonesia, as the user asked: a Language row on the settings page (each language by its own name) switches every word on screen at once, and it is kept between sessions; the settings page, from the title or the pause menu, is the only place to change it. The agent wrote the translation. (2026-10-21)
- `[Decision]` The only animals are bugs at ant scale (aphids herded like cattle, beetles, snails, ladybugs, crickets, water striders, butterflies, moths, fireflies). No hens, cats, birds or crabs. (2026-10-18)

Design pillars:
1. **No quest markers.** No arrows or checklists. The player matches clues from inspecting keepsakes (a station serial number, hands stopped at 10:17, a kind of stitching) with what residents say.
2. **Things connect people.** Every keepsake is a bridge between characters.
3. **Kindness over winning.** Wrong guesses get a puzzled reply or a new hint, never a penalty.
4. **A miniature fable.** An anthropomorphic ant colony lives among human leftovers, such as a walnut-shell cup or a station clock. Overridden: Bellwood is an ant planet with no humans; the clock and the cup are ant-made `[Decision]` (2026-10-18).
5. **Restoration minigames.** Tactile minigames (calibrating gears, stitching a patchwork, tuning a radio) with instant, penalty-free retries.

## World and story `[GDD]`

**Premise.** Satria spent eleven years as an express courier in the metropolis and comes home to the rural ant colony of Bellwood only to settle the deed and sell the late parents' house (Ibu Ratih and Daniel, Ayah Satria) to a city property agent. A brass bell leads Satria to The Shop That Opens Once: Marlow's old shop in the roots of the banyan tree by the station. It opens only from 00:00 to 00:30, for those with unfinished business. For seven nights Marlow gives Satria one relic box a night.

**Bellwood** is built from natural materials: twigs, roots, walnut shells and leaves. It has four zones joined by footpaths, with no loading screens between them:

Paths: West–Centre, Centre–East and Centre–South.

**Nine interiors** can be entered: Satria's childhood home, Marlow's shop, Helen's tea stall, Mender's forge, Baker's bakery, Sam's archive and vault No. 7, Theo's studio and darkroom, Nora's loom house, and Arlo's salt store.

**Lore.** Twenty years before the story, in 1952, a great storm and flash flood hit the Bellwood estuary. It brought down the south bridge and cut the railway for good. At 10:17 that night the last steam train was in danger. Satria's parents and the village elders gave up their tickets to move to the city to help: they led the bridge repairs and sewed warm clothes for the children. Traces of that kindness are left in the relics in Marlow's shop.

**Story arc:**
1. **Inciting incident.** Satria arrives at dusk with the sale contract from the city agent. That night Marlow's shop appears.
2. **Rising action (Nights 1–6).** Satria receives a relic each night, restores it through a minigame and returns it, and slowly understands why the parents stayed.
3. **Climax (Night 7).** Every lantern is out and the shelves are empty. Satria receives the family's walnut music box and Ratih's last letter.
4. **Ending (Day 8).** In the square, before the agent and the whole village, Satria tears up the contract, refuses the city's compensation, stays and reopens the village post office. Time in Bellwood flows again.

## Core loop and rules `[GDD]`

**One night, one keepsake:**
1. **Night: receive the relic.** At the midnight bell Satria visits the shop and receives a keepsake and a vague first hint. It goes into the courier bag.
2. **Inspect it.** In the bag the player turns the keepsake to see its front, back and sides (initials, a year, cracks, a scent). Clues go into Notes automatically.
3. **Morning to midday: explore and talk.** Satria walks the four zones, watches residents and shows them the keepsake.
4. **Afternoon: the empathy minigame.** A key witness opens a minigame (mend a stitch, tune a radio, clean a lens). Solving it unlocks the memory that names the owner.
5. **Dusk: the handover.** Satria gives the keepsake to its owner, a reconciliation cutscene plays, and one of the shop's seven lanterns goes out for good.

**Time of day.** A day has four phases: morning, midday, dusk and night. They advance with the player's progress, not with a clock. Each phase changes the light, the music and what the residents are doing. The HUD shows the day and phase on a small dial, never a digital time.

**Mechanics:**
- **Exploration.** The whole map is open from the start. Interactive things show a speech-bubble icon with three dots. What a place offers depends on the time of day and the clues found.
- **Dialogue.** A dialogue box at the bottom shows the speaker's portrait and name. After the opening lines the player picks one of two replies, and while carrying a keepsake can show it or say goodbye.
- **Bag and Notes.** Keepsakes on the left with a short description, Notes on the right. A notification at the top of the screen marks each new item or clue.
- **Clues** come from inspecting keepsakes and from minigames. They point to the next person or place. A keepsake can be handed over only after its required minigames and conversations are done.
- **Minigames.** Each fits the resident who offers it. Witnesses with clues carry no marker. Some minigames ask the player to remember earlier conversations. The GDD plans 14 or more.
- **Daily life at home.** Resting and writing the journal. Each night closes with a journal page on what Satria learned.

**Rules:**
- **Win:** finish all seven nights, open the secret drawer of Ratih's sewing table on Night 7, read her letter, and tear up the contract on the morning of Day 8.
- **No lose state:** no health, no game over, no permanent failure.
- **Minigame failure:** a friendly joke and an instant retry (R), with no penalty and no lost progress.
- **Wrong person:** a natural refusal, village gossip, or a hint toward the right witness.
- **Rewards:** no money or XP. Rewards are emotional: Notes, and the nightly journal page.

## UI `[GDD]`

The UI is minimal: no health bar, timer, minimap or quest markers. Panels are warm cream `#FFFAF0` with dark brown text `#2A1B22`.

- **Screens:** the title (Begin, Continue when a save exists, Quit on desktop only); exploration; the pause menu (Keep playing, Sound on/off, Back to the title); the bag; close inspection; dialogue; full-screen story cards with a lantern at the start and end of each night; minigames. Progress saves automatically at the end of each night.
- **HUD:** the bag icon top right; notifications top centre for 4 seconds; an interaction bubble over nearby things; an object's name next to the cursor; the dialogue box (moves to the top if it would cover a character), with a portrait that talks and blinks, the speaker's name, text typed at 50 characters a second and a "next" arrow; choices; a pixel cursor; and the day-and-phase dial top left.
- **Bag:** a 400x230 panel. Keepsake slots and their names on the left, Notes on the right, and "E or Esc to close".
- **Type:** Pixelify Sans for titles, chapter cards, place names and speaker names. DM Sans for dialogue, item descriptions and Notes. Both are already in `presentation/fonts/`.
- **Icons:** 16x16 and 24x24 with a thin dark-brown outline. A "..." bubble over people, a "?" magnifier over things to inspect, sun, lantern and moon phase icons, and a satchel with a gold dot when something new arrives.

## Audio `[GDD]`

All of it is synthesized or recorded from scratch `[Decision]`, as the Godot build did with `presentation/audio.js`.

- **Music:** 60–80 BPM, organic instruments: upright piano (Satria and home), nylon guitar (village days), celesta and music box (the shop and the keepsakes), low cello (confessions). Music cross-fades between zones and phases. Six planned tracks: *Quiet Station at Dawn*, *Bellwood Marketplace*, *The Cliff of Lingering Waves*, *Mother's Loom and Memories*, *The Shop at Midnight* and *Uncut Letter* (the epilogue and credits).
- **Footsteps** on six surfaces: wet soil, creaking boards, gravel, wet sand, grass and tiles.
- **Object sounds:** a watch ticking, cloth, the 8mm shutter, a key, a music box spring, a lantern going out.
- **Ambience by phase:** sparrows in the morning, a murmuring market at midday, waves and wind at dusk, crickets and dew at night.
- **Voices:** no voice acting. Each character has their own pitch of short dialogue blips: calm and middling for Satria, high and quick for Pip, low and heavy for Marlow. A soft chime marks a new clue, and a warm low note marks a lantern going out.

## Glossary

- **Keepsake** (in the GDD, *benda peninggalan* or *relik*): the object Marlow hands out each night. It belongs to a resident's unfinished story.
- **Night** (a chapter): one keepsake, from receiving it at midnight to handing it over at the next dusk.
- **10:17:** the time the 1952 storm cut the railway. The station's tower clock, the shop's pendulum clock and the Night 1 watch are frozen there.
- **Lantern:** one of the seven in the shop. One goes out for each resolved night.
- **Notes:** the clue list inside the courier bag.
- **Day phase:** morning, midday, dusk or night, which moves with the story.
- **No quest markers:** no arrows, waypoints or checklists. Players find each owner by observation and conversation.
